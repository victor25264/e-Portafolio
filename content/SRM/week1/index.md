#### Security and Risk Management
+ Risk management must ensure all parties have the same understanding of risk
+ Encompasses three processes: risk assessment, risk mitigation, and evaluation and assessment.
+ Helps balance the operational and economic costs of protective measures and achieve gains.


##### Definitions
+ Risk: has had many definitions like “possibility of an event that reduces values “, “net negative impact of exercising a vulnerability considering probability and impact”, and others. RISK = Threat x Vulnerability x Asset
+ Threats: A Potential cause of an unwanted incident
+ Vulnerabilities: weakness of an asset or control that can be exploited
+ PDCA mandate: Plan, do, check, and act


##### Risk identification:
+ There is a need to evaluate risk. There are different ways, like:
    + Risk notification mailboxes
    + Threat workshops
    + Business impact assessment
    + Gap analysis
    + Security audits
+ Information that helps:
    + System information
    + Threats
    + Vulnerabilities
    + Controls
    + Impact
+ After identification, risks must be analyzed.


##### Assessment Types:
+ Quantitative: Use of statistical or historical data to evaluate and assign weight to risk. Involves the use of probability and game theory.
+ Qualitative: Asking subject matter experts to evaluate risk as low, medium, or significant. Used when data is not available or there is no appropriate training or skills to perform a qualitative assessment.


##### Mitigation phase:
+ Eliminate
+ Tolerate
+ Reduce or Mitigate
+ Transfer
+ Avoidance


##### Control Categories 
+ Technical controls
+ Management controls
+ Operational controls


##### Common standards 

+ Open FAIR: Produced by the Open Group. Factor Analysis of Information Risk consists of an open risk taxonomy and open risk analysis.
+ OCTAVE: Operationally Critical threat, Asset, and Vulnerability evaluation Framework. Mix of Information Security Evaluation, CMU’s Software Engineering Institute, and Information Security Risk Management.

#### Collaborative Discussion

##### Initial Post

Hancock et al. (2024) explain that the introduction of technology into human rights investigations can pose challenges by introducing new errors and biases, as well as data misinterpretation, which can be challenging to detect when there are no “boots on the ground.” Additionally, data collection can raise concerns about individuals’ privacy and freedom, as many organizations fail to adhere to appropriate legal and ethical guidelines, leading to data misuse (Ahmed & Ahammed 2024). On the other hand, many technological solutions are developed without thinking of the vulnerable people they are meant to protect. This is demonstrated in multiple studies, where authors propose solutions that require multiple technologies and communication devices to ensure correct functionality, for instance, blockchain, mesh networks, and satellite communication (Connolly et al., 2023; Mohiqi, 2024). It is essential to note that some authors are aware of the accessibility limitations but still do not work directly with the victims they focus on, leaving critical information needed to develop feasible solutions. In summary, technology introduces new risks to human rights investigations by failing to accurately identify the problems and constraints victims face. New technology must consider these factors to ensure effectiveness in addressing issues.



#### Reflexion
+ Risk management is a crucial process to ensure business systems work securely. But, because there are different definitions of risks, it can be complicated to work on this. I have experienced this when talking with project managers who have a definition of risk different from mine. Finally, the different assessment frameworks presented in this unit can be applied to an enterprise, but micro or small businesses will have a hard time finding the time and resources for this.
