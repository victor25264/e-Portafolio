#### Application of Threat Modelling and Management Techniques


##### System security engineering
+ Ross et. al (2022): "helps to ensure that the appropriate security principles, concepts, methods, and practices are applied during the system life cycle to achieve stakeholder objectives for the protection of assets"
+ ISO (2025) defines a system as: "a set of interacting elements (i.e., system elements) organized to achieve 
one or more stated purposes"
+ Ross et. al (2022): "Systems security engineering delivers systems deemed adequately secure by stakeholders"

##### Risk identification
- Sutton (2021) "To determine the threats or hazards that could cause loss or damage to an information asset"
- Sutton (2021): "To identify any vulnerabilities exhibited by the information asset and to determine the possible impact or consequences to the information asset."

---


#### Reflexion
+ System security engineering must be part of every process throughout the development lifecycle. Sadly, I've seen some projects where security is an afterthought, leaving many vulnerabilities that attackers will try to exploit. For instance, there are many breaches that occur because VPNs lack MFA, even though it is widely known that MFA stops many attacks. On the other hand, given the different threats and vulnerabilities emerging, legacy systems are unable to defend against them. To me, this means that before, the threat model did not consider internet-based attacks or similar ones. For example, in operational technology, most of the legacy systems don't use encryption to communicate or are vulnerable to brute-force attacks. Identical to what Windows systems were vulnerable to back in the day, but in OT it is not possible to update these systems due to cost and compatibility constraints.
