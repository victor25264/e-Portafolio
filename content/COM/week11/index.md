#### AI and Cloud Computing

##### The Competitive Relationshio Between Cloud Computing and Generative AI
+ GenAI providers need cloud providers to train, run and deploy their GenAI solutions 
+ Cloud providers see GenAI providers as a business driver to grow
+ Cloud providers compete by adapting their infrastructures and services to GenAI needs
+ GenAI providers compete by frequently releasing models and applications
+ Relationship
  + Infrastructure (IaaS)
    + GenAI providers need this infrastructure to train, run and deploy their machine-learning models
    + GenAI providers scale up or down their storage and computing resources
    + Hyperscalers support GenAI providers financially through exclusive or strategic partnerships
    + The GenAI partner and their customers cannot easily switch to alternative cloud infrastructures.
    + Competition risks arise from discrimination in the supply of IT equipment by dominant IT providers
  + The platform level (PaaS) 
    + Cloud providers supplying PaaS enable their customers to access software and tools to develop their applications
    + Cloud providers and GenAI providers thus compete for the provision of AI tools, with potential competition risks
  + Software (SaaS)
    + Cloud providers supplying SaaS enable cloud customers to provide applications to their users via any connected device
+ Competition risks
  + Potential concentration issues 
    +  GenAI providers use mostly hyperscalers, increasing the trend toward concentration in the hands of the hyperscalers
    + smaller cloud providers might not be able to compete with hyperscalers at global level because they might lack the investment capabilities and infrastructure
    +  becomes harder for smaller providers and newcomers to compete in and to enter concentrated markets
    +  prone to anticompetitive practices arising from abuse of dominance and collusion because of their oligopolistic natures
  + Potential anticompetitive practices
    + relate to discrimination in the supply of IT equipment by dominant IT providers and interoperability obstacles
    + dominant IT providers might discriminate amongst its customers how it allocates it supply
    + Cloud providers might have the ability and incentive to limit the interoperability of GenAI solutions with other cloud providers
    + Cloud providers might have the ability and incentive to use data from GenAI providers and their customers, which might have an exploitative effec
    + Self-preferencing of cloud services over those of third parties
    + Cloud providers might tie the provision of non-dominant cloud services to the provision of related dominant services
    +  Cloud providers might have the ability and incentive to implement bundling strategies, with potential exclusionary effects
  + Competition law and regulatory responses
    + Competition law and regulatory responses
      + DMA requires gatekeepers to comply with a list of obligations addressing some practices deemed anticompetitive by the legislators
      + Data Act, meanwhile, requires cloud providers to comply with a list of obligations to address some practices deemed anticompetitive by the legislators

##### Reflexion

+ Some AI challenges can be solved using cloud computing, leveraging its scalability and agility. But there is a significant amount of energy and data center capacity under development that could reduce the expansion of AI. Additionally, the cost of using AI is unresolved, as companies are still losing money with subscription plans for users. There is a lack of understanding of how prices will change over time and how this will affect how people use these tools.
